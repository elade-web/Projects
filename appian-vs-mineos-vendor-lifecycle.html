Appian to MineOS: vendor onboarding and offboarding parity

Appian is a low-code BPM platform, so the client's vendor onboarding is an app someone built on it. MineOS is a packaged third-party risk product. Out of the box it covers the privacy, security and risk part of the vendor lifecycle. The Integration Builder connects procurement, ERP and IT systems. Mine builds those flows and the client configures them.

Bottom line

Where MineOS wins

Prebuilt vendor, DPIA, TIA, DORA, NIS2 and offboarding templates. External vendor collaboration, AI autofill from trust-center data and Panorays cyber ratings. Shadow-IT discovery and ROPA/data-map linkage. None of this has to be built or maintained.

Where we close it with Integration Builder

Intake from procurement tools (Zip, Coupa, ServiceNow, Jira). Push approved vendors to the ERP vendor master. Ticketing, Slack and Teams notifications. Offboarding hand-offs to IT and finance.

Where Appian may be ahead

A free-form BPMN designer and multi-level approval chains with SLA timers. Native ERP and RPA. Any sanctions, KYB or banking checks they built. Confirm what the client actually uses before treating any of these as a gap.

Onboarding in MineOS

Request Build

Requester submits in Zip, Coupa, ServiceNow or Jira. A flow creates the vendor in MineOS by domain and attaches the request as a form submission. Requesters can also use the in-portal request.

Enrich and triage Native

Catalog enrichment and AI autofill fill in certifications, trust center and data types. Panorays adds the cyber rating.

Assess Native

The TPRM trigger or a rule creates the right templates for the request, such as Vendor and DPIA, or DORA for ICT providers. The vendor is invited to answer in the collaborator view.

Review and decide Native

Any rejected assessment auto-rejects the vendor. Completed ones move it to In evaluation. The owner approves.

Hand off Build

SystemStatusChange webhook → flow notifies the requester, creates the supplier in the ERP and opens a provisioning ticket.

Monitor Partial

Review dates and reminders, Reassessment template, ongoing Panorays rating.

Offboarding in MineOS

Trigger Build

Contract end or termination in the procurement tool or ERP calls the API. Or the owner moves the vendor to Trash in MineOS, which requires a justification.

Offboarding assessment Native

The Offboarding template covers data return or deletion, access removal and certificates of destruction. A rule on state change can create it automatically. Mine configures this rule; it isn't on by default.

Revoke and close Build

A flow removes the app in Okta or Entra, closes the supplier in the ERP and opens IT and finance tickets.

Retain evidence Native

The vendor stays in Trash with who, when and why. Assessments and the audit log are kept, and the vendor can be restored if re-engaged.

Update records Native

The ROPA and data map reflect the vendor's removal.

What we can build with Integration Builder

Integration Builder is MineOS's embedded iPaaS (built on Prismatic). Flows start from a webhook, a schedule or a MineOS event, and call the MineOS public API and third-party systems. Mine's team builds the flows. The client deploys and configures them from the Automations page in MineOS.

Webhook in · procurement tool

Procurement intake to MineOS

A new request in Zip, Coupa, Ariba, ServiceNow or Jira creates the vendor and attaches the request answers. It then fires the TPRM trigger so the right assessments open.

SystemStatusChange · Approved

Approved vendor to ERP

Creates or updates the supplier in SAP, Oracle, NetSuite or Coupa. It writes the ERP ID back and notifies the requester.

SystemStatusChange · Rejected

Rejection feedback loop

Closes the procurement request with the reason and notifies the requester in Slack or Teams.

TprmTrigger · any

Approvals in ServiceNow or Jira

Opens approval tasks for security, legal and finance in the client's ticketing tool. It can route by business unit or tier. The flow writes the outcome back to MineOS.

Schedule · nightly

Screening hook

Calls the client's sanctions or credit provider for new vendors and records the result on the vendor. This is a custom flow, and the client supplies the provider and credentials.

Schedule · monthly

Tiered reassessment

Finds vendors due for review by tier and creates Reassessment assessments. The vendor contact is invited automatically.

Webhook in · contract end

Offboarding kickoff

A contract end date in the CLM or ERP moves the vendor to Trash and creates the Offboarding assessment.

Offboarding completed

Access and supplier shutdown

Deprovisions the app in Okta or Entra and blocks the supplier in the ERP. It opens IT and finance close-out tickets.

Webhook in · Appian

Migration and coexistence

Back-loads Appian vendor records into MineOS through the API. During a phased cutover, Appian can keep running procurement steps while MineOS owns risk.

Questions for the client

  1. Was the Appian app custom-built, a partner app (such as KPMG TPRM) or Appian Vendor Management? Who maintains it?
  2. Walk us through one vendor from request to ERP. Which steps are risk and compliance, and which are procurement or finance?
  3. How do vendors interact today: a Portal with links, Appian accounts, or email only?
  4. How are approvals routed (by spend, risk tier or business unit), and are there SLAs or escalations?
  5. Which systems are integrated, and in which direction? For example ERP, procurement, sanctions screening, DocuSign, ServiceNow, Okta.
  6. Do they screen for sanctions, KYB or credit, or collect banking and tax data, inside Appian?
  7. What does offboarding look like today? Who triggers it, and what evidence do they keep?
  8. What volume do they handle: vendors onboarded per year, active vendors, and reassessment cadence?
  9. What triggered the review: cost, maintenance, missing TPRM content, or vendor experience?

Positioning

  • Compare against the app they built, not the whole Appian platform. Appian can do almost anything if someone develops it.
  • Lead with what they'd stop building: questionnaires, the vendor portal, AI autofill, cyber ratings, discovery and audit evidence.
  • Position MineOS as the system of record for third-party risk. The procurement tool or ERP keeps finance onboarding, and Integration Builder connects them.
  • If they rely on a deep BPMN process with multi-tier approvals, propose running approvals in ServiceNow or Jira through flows. Don't claim MineOS has a native equivalent.
  • Don't promise sanctions or KYB screening, banking or tax collection, or RPA as MineOS features.

Check internally before the call

  • The exact Prismatic connector set for the client's ERP and procurement stack.
  • Whether the client can self-serve the Integration Builder designer or only deploys flows Mine builds.
  • Roadmap for public API writes to custom fields, review status and risks.
  • Rule engine: is it configured by customers or only by Mine staff? Is the auto-offboarding rule available?
  • How external collaborators sign in (magic link or password).
